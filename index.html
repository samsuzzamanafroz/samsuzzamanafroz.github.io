About Me

Ph.D. candidate at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), Mumbai, working at the intersection of fundamental physics, astrophysics, and cosmology with gravitational waves. I combine theory, large-scale data analysis and modern statistical methods to turn observations from gravitational-wave detectors and cosmological surveys into robust physical insight.

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), Mumbai, where my research focuses on fundamental physics, astrophysics, and cosmology using gravitational-wave observations. My work lies at the interface of theory, data analysis, and statistical inference, with a strong emphasis on extracting robust physical constraints from complex and noisy datasets.

My research spans strong- and weak-field tests of General Relativity, multi-messenger cosmology and the nature of dark energy, the astrophysical populations and formation channels of binary black holes, and searches for dark matter through gravitational-wave bursts. I work with data from current and future gravitational-wave detectors, including LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA, LISA, Cosmic Explorer, and the Einstein Telescope, and combine these with electromagnetic probes such as DESI and the Pantheon+ supernova sample.

Methodologically, I use Bayesian inference, including hierarchical modeling, nested sampling and Gaussian processes, together with parametric and non-parametric reconstructions, to extract robust and well-calibrated results from complex, noisy data. I have contributed to gravitational-wave data analysis and cosmological inference efforts within the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA Collaboration. I am also increasingly interested in machine learning and in how it can complement these methods for gravitational-wave data.

More broadly, my work is motivated by the use of rigorous statistical methods to address fundamental questions in physics and cosmology, and I am interested in extending these quantitative approaches to a wide range of data-intensive scientific and industry problems.

Seeing the data

Data Visualizations

I love creating beautiful and informative visualizations of gravitational-wave data and astrophysical phenomena. These plots help communicate complex scientific concepts and showcase the power of gravitational-wave astronomy. The figure below puts every LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA detection with published source parameters from 2015 to 2025 into a single picture, and the interactive event catalogue lets you explore the full Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog yourself.

This visualization presents gravitational-wave detections from the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA (LVK) observing runs between 2015 and 2025, using every event with published source parameters from the GWOSC Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog. Each event corresponds to a compact binary merger, plotted versus the time of observation (horizontal axis) and the component masses (vertical axis, in solar masses). For each detection, the two markers connected by a vertical line represent the individual masses of the binary components, illustrating the mass ratio of the system. Events are color-coded by their luminosity distance, highlighting sources ranging from nearby mergers to events at cosmological distances. The upper axis shows the cumulative detection number, emphasizing the rapid growth of the gravitational-wave catalog as detector sensitivity improved across observing runs.
